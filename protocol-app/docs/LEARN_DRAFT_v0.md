## 0 · The on-ramp piece — R25, drafted

*This is the one-time teaching R25 promoted to the front: fair warning, once and

nearly all of them Kevin verbatim. Shape (screen at onboarding vs. dismissible

> **Read this once. It's the only warning you'll get, so it's a real one.**
>
> Most of this work is uncomfortable. Some of it genuinely hurts. Rolling out
> the armpit or the back of the shoulder is intense; finding a nerve that runs
> the length of your arm is part of finding what's been asleep. If your daily
> life has sucked for years, you already know nobody fixed it by being gentle
> with it. So here's the honest pitch: this is going to suck, and here is what
> you get for it — easier breathing, the weight of your head coming off your
> neck, joints that stop aching one notch at a time, a body that starts lining
> back up. That trade is worth naming out loud, because you're the one making
> it.
>
> The app will not tell you, mid-rep, which pain is the good kind. Nobody can
> call that for someone else's body — learning to read those signals is the
> actual skill this whole thing teaches. What it will do is sequence you: the
> first releases you meet are the ones that can't destabilise a joint, and the
> deeper work arrives when the parts around it can hold. That's the safety
> mechanism. Order, not interruptions.
>
> Three signals are different, and are never push-through moments: sharp pain
> that shoots or stabs, a joint that gives way or slides, and going lightheaded.
> Those mean stop and look, every time.

## 1 · Why the day is shaped like this

*Source: FRAMEWORK's laws, all previously ruled. This section is the "why we do
the things we do" half of the brief, told as reasons rather than rules.*

- **Why there's a wake block:** the morning's first movement reverses whatever
  position the body held for hours (unwind-the-night). As of 1 Sep it rotates by
  your sleeping position, because a side sleeper's night and a back sleeper's
  night leave different things to undo.
- **Why every block has a sixty-second floor:** missing part one never forfeits
  part two. On a bad morning, one minute of rocking IS the block.
- **Why release never travels alone:** the session that opens a region ends by
  loading it — otherwise the new range is borrowed, not owned. (Pairing law;
  visible on every dealt session as "loads X after opening it".)
- **Why missed days don't stack up:** there is no backlog and no guilt
  mechanic. Rotation just resumes where coverage is thinnest, which is also why
  the app can honestly say a missed week costs you a week and nothing more.
- **Why something you keep skipping comes back:** the medicine drop. Once or
  twice a week the day includes one avoided-but-needed thing. One honest tap
  skips it; it returns, because avoidance is information about where the work is.

## What Learn is not (already ruled, kept here so it stays true)

- No meters, no streaks, no commentary about the person (content law 2).
- Epistemic status travels with every claim (law 5): established / exploratory
  / traditional-claim-noted-as-claim.
- No per-item warnings (R25). The on-ramp piece is the warning, once.
- Connection cards drip one at a time; notice-lines reinforce on ordinary
  cards. Learn is pull, not push.

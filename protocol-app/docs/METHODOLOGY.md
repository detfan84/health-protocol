# METHODOLOGY — how this work is done, and why

*Draft, 23 Aug 2026. **Written for Kevin to mark up**, not to be agreed with as it
stands. Every principle below was extracted from something he already wrote — the 33
body-work cards in `src/data/bodywork.js`, `FRAMEWORK.md`, the content laws in
`Protocol_App_Roadmap_v1_7.md`, ruling R25, and the education backlog. Nothing here is
new doctrine; where the wording is Claude's rather than his, the principle says so.*

*Provenance per canon 3.7: **[quoted]** is his words, lifted. **[derived]** is his idea in
Claude's words, with the source cited — strike or rewrite freely. **[synthesis]** means the
principle is real in the cards but was never stated as a principle anywhere.*

---

## What this document is for

Two jobs, and the second one is the reason it exists.

**One:** to have the thesis written down in one place instead of spread across 33 cards,
four documents and a year of sessions.

**Two — the leverage:** every item in the app is supposed to tell you *why* it is there.
Right now 162 of the 258 items in the library carry a coaching cue in the `why` field
("Drive knee up, skip with purpose, stay tall") because they came out of a generic
exercise database. Writing 258 real whys by hand is a job nobody finishes. Writing
**twenty principles** and pointing each item at the two or three it serves is a job that
finishes — and every later improvement to a principle lifts every item pointing at it.

So the principles below are numbered and given ids on purpose. They are going to become
data.

## The founding insight

### P1 · Counterforce
**id** `counterforce`

> Two years of serious protocol work underdelivered because it pushed the gas with the
> brake on. The lesson wasn't "wrong supplements" — it was "wrong foundation," and more
> precisely: counterforce. **[quoted — roadmap v1.7, thesis]**

Systems push on each other. A door you are working to close stays open when something
unaddressed keeps shoving it. This is why the app is not a checklist: skipping a piece
is not a lower score, it can be *the reason the work you are doing will not hold*. The
whole structure of the day arc, the pairing law, and the module library are this one idea
made operational.

*Shows up everywhere. The clearest single instance is the breathing section: everything
downstream stays stuck while the ribs stay flared.*

## How tissue actually changes

### P2 · Release opens, load keeps
**id** `release-then-load`

> The flexor let go; the glute has to take the job. **[quoted — `hip` card, load]**

A muscle that finally releases has no job to go back to, and the body will hand the work
straight back to whatever it was already using. Release is never scheduled alone: the
session that opens a region ends by loading it, and the next few days echo activation in
the new range. This is FRAMEWORK's pairing law, and it is the hard one — the composer
treats it as a constraint, not a preference.

*Shows up in:* `hip` · `glute` · `feet` · `calf` · `ham` · `pec` · `lat` · `thor` ·
`neck` · `quad`
*Law:* FRAMEWORK pairing law (hard)

### P3 · Wait for the guard to let go
**id** `wait-for-the-guard`

> Sink onto it slowly. Wait for the guard to let go, then a little more.
> **[quoted — `hip` card, release]**

What holds a range shut is usually a nervous system deciding it is not safe to open it,
not a tissue that is physically short. Force argues with the guard and confirms it was
right. Pressure plus time plus a long exhale asks instead — and the release, when it
comes, is the body granting permission rather than the tissue giving way. This is why
every release hold runs extended-exhale breathing underneath it.

*Shows up in:* `hip` · `glute` · `neck` · `rg-cr` · `br-exhale`

### P4 · Range you own, not range you borrow
**id** `range-you-own`

> Range gained this way tends to still be there tomorrow. Range gained from passive
> holding usually is not. **[quoted — `rg-cr` card, notice]**

Loaded lengthening — contract-relax, eccentrics, end-range strength — buys range the
nervous system will let you keep, because you have proved you can control it. Passive
holding buys range you have to keep re-earning. *"Calves get stronger and longer at the
same time. This is how you get range that holds instead of range you have to keep
re-earning."* (`rg-ecc`)

*Shows up in:* the whole `range` section · `rg-cr` · `rg-ham` · `rg-calf` · `rg-ecc` ·
`rg-squat`

### P5 · Tight and weak are different problems
**id** `tight-is-not-weak`

> A weak calf needs strength, not length. **[quoted — `calf` card, careful, generalised]**

The same complaint — this side feels wrong — has two opposite fixes, and picking the
wrong one makes it worse. A muscle that is short and guarded wants release and then load.
A muscle that is long, weak and overstretched wants load and nothing else; releasing it
is releasing the only thing holding the joint together. Telling them apart is a skill,
and it is one of the things the app teaches rather than decides.

*Shows up in:* `calf` · `lat` · `neck`

### P6 · Stability is the fix; depth is not the goal
**id** `stability-over-depth`

> If a hip clicks, catches or feels like it slides, release is the opener and
> stability is the fix — do not chase depth.
> **[quoted — `hip` card, careful, generalised]**

For a joint that clicks, catches or slides, more range is not the win — it is the risk.
Release enough to let the joint sit where it should, then spend the effort on the active
support that keeps it there. The contract-relax method carries the same rule at its
sharpest: *"Only on restricted muscle — never used to push a joint that subluxes further
into range."* (`rg-cr`)

*Shows up in:* `hip` · `lat` · `rg-cr` · `rg-ham` (knee softly bent, never locked)
*Law:* FRAMEWORK law 10, capacity gates

### P7 · The position comes from underneath
**id** `from-underneath`

> Do not force shoulders-back-chest-out. That retraction is the move that reproduces
> thoracic-outlet symptoms in people who have them — let the position come from
> underneath. **[quoted — `pec` card, careful, generalised]**

Good posture held by effort is a second problem stacked on the first. What you want is
the position arriving on its own because the thing that was pulling you out of it let go
and the thing that should hold you in it came back online. Every time the app says
"posture," this is what it means — and *"Standing tall stops being effort"* (`thor`) is
what success sounds like.

*Shows up in:* `pec` · `thor` · `lat` · `neck` · `aw-posture`

### P8 · Breath is upstream
**id** `breath-upstream`

> Breathing is upstream of most of it. Everything else on this list is downstream effect.
> **[quoted — `breath` section note, generalised]**

The rib position sets the diaphragm's position, which sets what the low back and the deep
core can do, which sets what the hips are compensating for. Working the downstream effect
while the ribs stay flared is the counterforce problem in miniature. It is also the
fastest lever available: *"Tissue lets go faster under release when you are doing this
than when you are not. Test it on a spot you know well."* (`br-exhale`)

*Shows up in:* the whole `breath` section · `ribs` · `rg-squat` · every release hold
*Note:* `rb-diaph` records the anatomical version — the diaphragm and the psoas are
continuous tissue, *"your best release and your main symptom are literally continuous tissue."*

## How you find out what is actually wrong

### P9 · Where it hurts is not where it is from
**id** `referral`

> If your hamstring range improves from this when stretching has not moved it, the
> restriction was never muscular. **[quoted — `nv-sciatic` card, notice]**

Pain refers. Tissue that will not let go is often reporting a problem somewhere else, and
sometimes it is not muscle at all. The practical form of this is a test rather than a
theory: try the other tissue's tool and see whether the thing moves. Nerve glides are the
cleanest example — different tissue, different rules, and *"More is not better here."*

*Shows up in:* `nerve` section · `nv-sciatic` · `nv-median`
*Roadmap:* decision 37, reverse referral lookup (exploratory tier)

### P10 · Notice is the instrument
**id** `notice-is-the-instrument`

> Individual toe control is a direct readout of the nerve pathway you are trying to
> rebuild. Watching it go from impossible to easy is the clearest progress marker in this
> entire document. **[quoted — `bl-toes` card, notice]**

Every card carries a **notice** field, and it is not decoration — it is how you find out
whether the thing worked, without a device and without waiting for a number. "The ground
feels more detailed." "Ribs stop flaring." "The urge to crack drops." Learning to read
those is the deepest skill the app teaches, and it is what makes every later judgment
possible.

**[synthesis]** *— the `notice` field is on all 33 cards but is never named as a
principle anywhere. It is arguably the centre of the whole method.*

*Shows up in:* all 33 body-work cards
*Law:* FRAMEWORK law 8, listen and lean; the awareness curriculum

### P11 · One number, honestly taken
**id** `one-real-measure`

> The number rising over weeks IS the thing you are trying to fix, and it is the only
> hard metric in this document. **[quoted — `br-co2` card, notice]**

Most of this work is read by feel, and feel drifts. A small number of measures are worth
taking properly — same time of day, same conditions, written down — precisely because
they do not drift. And the discipline is in the taking: *"If you are gasping afterwards
you held too long and the number is invalid."*

*Shows up in:* `br-co2` · knee-to-wall · forward fold · the 13 `selftest` items
*Roadmap:* decision 36, measured self-tests

## How much, how often, and what is actually dangerous

### P12 · Most of this is uncomfortable, and that is the work
**id** `pain-is-the-medium`

> This is going to suck, and here is why it is worth it. **[derived — R25, Kevin, 23 Aug]**

Rolling out the back of the armpit is intense. You will hit a nerve and feel it run down
the arm. For the people this is aimed at, pain is not an exception to daily life, it is
daily life — so an app that flinches at discomfort has nothing to offer them. What is
promised is not gentleness, it is that the discomfort buys something: easier breathing,
the weight of the head coming off the neck, joint pain slowly dissipating, feeling more
free.

**The app never decides for you which pain is which.** Nobody can call that for someone
else's body, and an app that warned before every item would be ignored before every item.
What it does instead: teach the difference once and properly, put the honest caution on
the card where the risk is real, and deal the safer work first.

*Ruling:* R25 — *"I don't want to be dictating in the moment what they should and
shouldn't be doing for their own best interest."*

### P13 · The signals that mean stop are specific
**id** `stop-signals`

> Numbness or shooting down the leg means move the ball, not push harder.
> **[quoted — `glute` card, careful]**

P12 says discomfort is the medium. This says the exceptions are nameable, and they are
not "it hurts":

- **Sharp**, as opposed to deep and intense. *"It should never be sharp."* (`rb-diaph`)
- **Numbness, tingling, or shooting** down a limb — nerve, not muscle. Reduce range.
  (`nv-sciatic`, `nv-median`, `glute`)
- **A joint giving way, sliding or catching** — that is P6's territory, not something to
  push through.
- **Lightheadedness** — stop, sit, breathe normally. (`br-9090`, `bl-prog`)
- **Joint pain as against muscle fatigue.** *"Jaw fatigue is expected; jaw pain is not."*
  (`aw-press`)

*Law:* FRAMEWORK law 10 — sharp pain, joint giving-way and lightheadedness are
stop-and-evaluate signals, never push-through moments.

### P14 · Dose is the safety mechanism
**id** `dose-is-safety`

> Start at half the volume you think you need and watch the following two days before
> adding any. **[quoted — `rg-ecc` card, careful]**

The choice is almost never "do it or don't." It is how much, how hard, how often — and
that is where safety actually lives, because it leaves the thing itself available. *"Daily
and light beats hard and occasional"* (`feet`). *"Tender because they're deconditioned, not
damaged"* (`feet`). A drill that is dangerous at ten reps is often the right drill at
three.

*Shows up in:* `feet` · `rg-ecc` · `nv-sciatic` · `aw-press` · `rb-inter`
*Roadmap:* the movement dial; decision 28, pacing profile

### P15 · Delayed is the operative word
**id** `delayed-response`

> Eccentrics cause real delayed soreness, and delayed is the operative word given the
> post-exertional picture. **[quoted — `rg-ecc` card, careful]**

The cost of a session does not always arrive during the session, and for this population
it often arrives a day or two later. That breaks the ordinary feedback loop everybody
learns to train by — you cannot judge a dose by how it felt at the time. What replaces it
is a longer look-back and a written record, which is the whole reason the app keeps one.

*Roadmap:* decision 28 (the lag; immediate ≠ delayed) · decision 33, activity log ·
the 48-hour look-back

### P16 · Say what a fair trial looks like before it starts
**id** `fair-test-up-front`

> Give it 2–3 months before judging whether it works. **[quoted — `aw-press`, dose]**
>
> Effects on sleep take months, not days — this is the slowest-acting thing in this
> document and also one of the highest-value. **[quoted — `aw-press`, notice]**

Some of this moves in a session. Some of it moves in a quarter. If the app does not say
which up front, then "I tried it and it didn't work" has no honest answer — and for
people who have been told for years that they did not try hard enough, an unfalsifiable
promise is not just sloppy, it is cruel.

*Law:* content law 3 — define the fair test up front. The answer to a fair trial that
underdelivered is adjustment or a clinician, never "try harder."

## How it becomes yours

### P17 · Shore up the weakest link
**id** `weakest-link`

> In personal development you lean into your strengths. In the body it is reversed — you
> shore up the weakest links so the whole system can get strong.
> **[derived — Kevin, Aug 20, roadmap education backlog]**

This doubles as the answer to "why all this glute work." The chain performs at the level
of its worst link, and the strong parts are already compensating for the weak ones —
which is what made them tight in the first place. Training what you are good at makes the
compensation better.

### P18 · The connection has to come back before it can be trained
**id** `connection-first`

> The lifts will be close to impossible at first. That is the point — it is a connection
> you do not have yet. **[quoted — `bl-toes` card, steps]**

A muscle that will not fire is not a strength problem yet. First the brain has to find it
again, which is a matter of attention and reps rather than load, and only then does
loading it mean anything. This is why the app asks you to notice where the work lands
rather than just counting reps — *"That is the foot-to-brain conversation you are trying
to restart."* (`bl-prog`)

*Roadmap:* Movement Activation Focus — bring dormant muscles back online, then
deliberately strengthen the brain-to-muscle pathway
*Law:* FRAMEWORK law 2, reciprocal pairing; the awareness curriculum

### P19 · Hang it on something you already do
**id** `attach-to-a-ritual`

> Fold it into brushing your teeth so you do not have to remember it separately.
> **[quoted — `aw-palate` card, dose]**

The failure mode of every home programme is remembering it. So the app does not ask you
to remember — it attaches work to a cue that already exists in your day and colonises the
dead time around it. *"Attach it to a fixed cue — after dinner, or while reading — rather
than relying on remembering."* (`vg-mat`)

*Law:* FRAMEWORK law 7, attach to existing rituals; the day arc's rise block and ambient
practices

### P20 · Everyday positions are the reps
**id** `positions-are-reps`

> **[derived — Movement Activation Focus seed, Aug 18; FRAMEWORK awareness layer]**

The set you do is not where most of the work happens. How you stand at the counter, how
you sit at the desk, where your tongue rests, whether your mouth is closed — those are
hours a day against a few minutes of exercise. The awareness layer is trained rather than
scheduled: stack up, reverse the joint, squeeze and hold, belly button to spine.

*Shows up in:* `aw-posture` · `br-nasal` · the awareness layer of the day arc

### P21 · A default you are rebuilding, not a rule you are passing
**id** `data-not-failure`

> Reaching for the mouth constantly is data, not failure — it points at nasal obstruction.
> **[quoted — `br-nasal` card, notice]**

Everything that does not go to plan is information about the system, and the app treats
it that way: a missed day is not a debt, an empty bottle is a reason to stop asking, a
break in nose breathing is a pointer at an airway. *"It is a default you are rebuilding,
not a rule you are passing or failing."*

*Law:* content law 2, teach everywhere and audit nowhere · decision 8a, the no-guilt law ·
FRAMEWORK law 5, self-healing not debt

## What is deliberately not here

- **Diagnosis, and treatment for named conditions.** Conditions for health, never cures
  for conditions (content law 4). Named therapeutic approaches live in get-evaluated prep.
- **Anything that decides for you in the moment.** See P12. The app teaches the
  distinction and then trusts you with it.
- **Cracking your own neck.** The one item in the whole corpus with a flat prohibition
  rather than a caution: *"Self-manipulating the cervical spine with lax connective tissue
  is the one item here with a serious downside — the vertebral arteries run through the
  joints you would be torquing."* (`neck`)
- **Mouth taping at night** for anyone who can reproduce their own airway collapse.
  *"That advice is everywhere in this space and it is specifically wrong"* — it removes the
  escape route. (`br-nasal`)

## What happens to this next

Once the principles are yours rather than mine, they become `src/content/principles.json`
— id, name, the line, the teaching — and every catalogue item gains a `principleIds`
list. The app then renders "why this is here" from the linked principles instead of from a
form cue, one edit to a principle improves every item pointing at it, and the composer
eventually gets a real answer to "what is this item *for*."

That is the leverage. Twenty-one principles against 258 items, and the number of items is
only going up.

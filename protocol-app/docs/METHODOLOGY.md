## Part 1 — The thesis

### The system is compensating, and has been for a long time

> Two years of serious protocol work underdelivered because it pushed the gas with the
> brake on. The lesson wasn't "wrong supplements" — it was "wrong foundation," and more
> precisely: counterforce. **[quoted — roadmap v1.7]**

Systems push on each other. A door you are working to close stays open while something
unaddressed keeps shoving it. Nothing here is a checklist item you can skip for a lower
score — skipping a piece can be *the reason the work you are doing will not hold*.

> You are not assembling random interventions. You are reverse-engineering an integrated
> system that has been compensating across the whole chain for decades. Each piece
> supports the others. That is why the work is slow, and why it is also tractable — every
> small improvement in any one piece makes the next piece more accessible.
> **[quoted — Footwear of Peace, 24 May, closing note]**

That last sentence is the reason to keep going, and it belongs somewhere a person can find
it on a bad week.

### The two claims, graded differently

The app claims two things and they do not carry the same weight, so they do not get the
same language:

- **Movement patterns and posture change.** Claimed.
- **Deeper autonomic restoration.** *"The working hypothesis, graded honestly in the
  evidence review."* **[quoted — FRAMEWORK]**

The evidence review is blunter still: the modifiable levers — exercise, slow breathing,
sleep — have real measurable autonomic benefit, and exercise reconditioning is the
best-evidenced treatment for orthostatic intolerance on record; but *"subclinical
sympathetic dominance is rising"* is plausible speculation, not fact. Content law 5 is
this paragraph turned into a rule: epistemic status travels with the claim.

## Part 2 — The chain

**This is what v1 missed entirely, and it is the spine of the method.**

### The chain, stated

> The lats anchor the rib cage for the diaphragm. The diaphragm trains the deep core. The
> deep core stabilizes the lumbar spine so the hip flexors can let go. The hip flexors
> releasing lets the glutes fire. The glutes firing reorganizes the femoral rotation so
> the knees track and the arches lift. The arches lifting feeds back up the chain.
> **[quoted — Footwear of Peace, 24 May]**

Read it twice. It runs downward and then closes upward, and that last clause is why the
feet are not the bottom of a list but a term in a circuit.

### Ground up

> Stand. Then walk. Then run. In that order. The footwear of peace is being built from the
> ground up. **[quoted — Footwear of Peace, 24 May]**

The foot is where the body meets the ground and where it finds out where it is; everything
above inherits whatever the foot reports. So the foot gets released, then *woken* — short
foot, toe control, arch doming without curling the toes — and then loaded under balance.
The progression is not more range, it is more control: *"over weeks you need less and less
arm support. That is the progression — not more depth, but how much of your own weight you
can hold down there."* (`rg-squat`)

### Where the gut sits in the chain

**[derived — health history layer 4, generalised; named as intent in the 23 Aug
dictation]**

This is the link every movement programme leaves out, and it explains why the deep core
will not answer when you call it.

When motility is slow — and being stuck in fight-or-flight is one reason it is slow —
material backs up and the abdominal cavity distends. Three consequences follow, and they
are mechanical rather than mysterious:

1. **The pelvic floor and lower core are held stretched.** Chronic distension
   over-stretches that musculature, and a muscle held long and slack cannot contract well.
   The source records the result plainly: *difficulty engaging the pelvic floor and lower
   core voluntarily.*
2. **The diaphragm loses its excursion.** Trying to take a deep breath against existing
   abdominal pressure adds pressure with nowhere to go. Shortness of breath here is
   mechanical, not pulmonary.
3. **Holding it in makes both worse.** Sustained core engagement to compress a distended
   abdomen drives pressure *upward* against the diaphragm and *downward* against the
   pelvic floor at the same time.

So the order matters: **empty out first, then go looking for the muscles.** Diet and
motility work are not a separate track running beside the movement work — for the lower
core, the pelvic floor and the hips they are *upstream of it*. Then find the drills that
let you identify those muscles, then keep diet and movement right so the momentum holds.

> The basketball in the belly is still one I am working on. It's so uncomfortable to try
> to relax into that. But it has helped my breathing. **[dictated 23 Aug]**

*Both halves of that ship: it is uncomfortable, he is still working on it, and it has still
helped. An app that only prints the second half is the kind of app this one exists not to
be.*

### The counterforce reading of the chain

Every link is somebody's stuck door. Release a hip flexor while the deep core is offline
because the abdomen is distended, and the flexor will be back by evening — not because the
release failed but because it was holding something.

## Part 3 — The operating rules

*From the Body Work Manual, Part 1 — "read once, apply everywhere." The closest thing to a
constitution the corpus has.* **[quoted, generalised]**

1. **"Tight" usually means "working," not "stuck."** In a hypermobile body a chronically
   tight muscle is often *guarding* a joint the ligaments cannot hold. Do not just attack
   it — work out what it is protecting, and strengthen that.
2. **Release opens the window; loading keeps it open.** Never release without re-loading
   the new range soon after. *"Release alone gets re-tightened, usually within hours."*
3. **Gentle beats aggressive.** No deep scraping, aggressive cupping or brute-force tools
   where tissue is fragile. Good discomfort, never sharp.
4. **Light load, strict form — never heavy.** Strengthening here is about the neural
   connection and motor control, not the weight. *"Heavy load just reinforces
   compensation."*
5. **Protect lax joints; do not push them to end range under load.** Work mid-range.
   End-range loaded stretching on a hypermobile joint makes it looser, which is the
   opposite of the goal.
6. **Noise is not damage.** Painless clicking and popping is normal for many bodies. Pain,
   swelling, locking, or a joint actually subluxing — *that* is what gets attention.

Two more the cards enforce that the manual does not list:

7. **The position comes from underneath.** *"Do not force shoulders-back-chest-out. That
   retraction is the move that reproduces thoracic-outlet symptoms in people who have
   them — let the position come from underneath."* (`pec`) Posture held by effort is a
   second problem stacked on the first; *"Standing tall stops being effort"* (`thor`) is
   what success sounds like.
8. **Do not dig at what is bracing for you.** *"A tight low back in a hypermobile person is
   usually compensating for weak deep core and unstable hips/pelvis. Digging at it removes
   the brace."* (Body Work Manual) Same where motor control is already fixing something:
   *"leave that area alone with tools."*

## Part 4 — The mechanisms

*Each of these is a different lever, and they are not interchangeable.*

### M1 · Release, then load
**id** `release-then-load`

> The flexor let go; the glute has to take the job. **[quoted — `hip` card]**

The non-negotiable one. *"Release opens the window; loading and balance keep it open.
Don't release without re-loading — that's the loop that wastes effort."* (Tailored
Movement Program) FRAMEWORK's pairing law is this as a hard composer constraint, which is
why `load` is a first-class field on a card rather than a note.

### M2 · Wait for the guard
**id** `wait-for-the-guard`

> Sink onto it slowly. Wait for the guard to let go, then a little more.
> **[quoted — `hip` card]**

What holds a range shut is usually a nervous system deciding it is not safe to open, not
tissue that is physically short. Force argues with the guard and confirms it was right.
Pressure, time and a long exhale ask instead — which is why every release hold runs
extended-exhale breathing underneath it: *"the muscle releases far faster when the nervous
system is parasympathetic-dominant. Aggressive holding-your-breath under release pressure
just keeps the system sympathetic."* (Footwear of Peace)

### M3 · Breath as a pneumatic force
**id** `breath-as-stretch` · **[dictated 23 Aug — stated nowhere else in the corpus]**

> Stretching to a position, holding it while taking a deep breath — the breath can expand
> and loosen up tight muscles and fascia, so when you exhale you are extending your ROM.
> Keep holding the position; several deep breaths will keep getting greater ROM.
> **[dictated 23 Aug]**

Here the breath is not a downregulator — it is **a force applied from the inside**. Held in
a position, the inhale inflates and broadens tissue from within; the exhale lets you settle
further in; the next breath starts from the new place. Range arrives in steps, one breath
at a time, with nobody pulling on anything.

Why it matters beyond the range itself: it lengthens tissue without ever putting a lax
joint at end range under external load, which rules 5 and 6 both forbid. The force is
internal and self-limiting.

The corpus already does this in one narrow place without naming it — `rb-inter` puts a soft
ball between two ribs and breathes the rib open against it, five breaths a spot. **That is
this mechanism applied to the intercostals.** Naming it generalises it to everything else.

> **Kevin — this is the section most likely to be wrong in its details, because it is
> written from three sentences.** Where does it apply and where does it not? Which
> positions? How many breaths before it stops giving? What tells you it is opening rather
> than just compressing? Does it change for fascia versus muscle?

### M4 · Contract–relax
**id** `contract-relax`

> Range gained this way tends to still be there tomorrow. Range gained from passive holding
> usually is not. **[quoted — `rg-cr` card]**

Into first tension — not end range, not pain — push against something immovable at 25–50 %,
let go completely, long exhale, ease into the range that opens. Three rounds. *"That is the
nervous system granting permission."* Two or three times a week per muscle: a training
stimulus, not a warm-up.

### M5 · Loaded lengthening
**id** `range-you-own`

> Calves get stronger and longer at the same time. This is how you get range that holds
> instead of range you have to keep re-earning. **[quoted — `rg-ecc` card]**

Eccentrics and end-range strength buy range the nervous system will let you keep, because
you have proved you can control it. *"Eccentric strength is what produces durable change in
hypermobile joints."* (Footwear of Peace)

### M6 · Flossing
**id** `flossing` · **[quoted — Body Work Manual Part 2, generalised]**

> The compression plus the tissue sliding under the band — and the rush of blood when you
> release — is the "floss." **The movement is the mechanism**; a band you don't move under
> does almost nothing.

Muscle bellies only. Never over bone, never over a tendon alone, never anywhere you cannot
then actively move. Short bouts — 60–90 seconds, then off, *not* the wrap-it-and-walk-around
version. Never to numbness; reposition immediately. Never on a lax joint at end range.

### M7 · Fascia
**id** `fascia` · **[thin — the largest hole in the corpus]**

Fascia is named as co-equal with muscle work: *fascia release + reconnection* is one of the
four Foundation modules, the Body Work Manual is subtitled *"your free-materials replacement
for a fascia/alignment course,"* and the vocabulary census counts three fascia targets beside
its forty-four muscles.

What the corpus actually contains about it:

- **The thoracolumbar fascia as the connector.** The lat *"attaches from the upper arm all
  the way down to the iliac crest via the thoracolumbar fascia. It is one of the few muscles
  that connects the upper body to the pelvis directly."* (Footwear of Peace)
- **Continuity, felt directly.** *"The psoas attaches into the same lumbar fascia — your best
  release and your main symptom are literally continuous tissue."* (`rb-diaph`)
- **Ball-on-foot fascia work** as an ambient practice (FRAMEWORK).
- **Fascia answers to breath**, per M3 — inflation from inside rather than traction from
  outside.

What is missing, and only Kevin can supply it: what makes fascial work *different in
practice* from muscle work — the timescales, why sustained low pressure rather than repeated
strokes, what "reconnection" means as against release, how a fascial restriction presents
differently from a tight muscle, and which of his own releases are fascial rather than
muscular. He named releasing *"the fascia around my chest/back/abdomen"* and *"fascia/lymph
fluid backup"* around the lats **[dictated 23 Aug]**; the lymphatic thread is a second hole,
and the lymphatic doc the roadmap references is not on this machine.

## Part 5 — Finding what is offline

### F1 · You cannot strengthen what you cannot find
**id** `find-it-first`

> I am practicing holding my lower core as I type this. These are muscles I couldn't "find"
> or identify how to contract them before I started this journey. That's part of finding
> what's offline and bringing it back online so you can actually target what's weak.
> **[dictated 23 Aug]**

The step every generic programme skips. Before a muscle is weak it is *absent* — the
connection is not there, and load applied to an absent muscle goes straight to whatever is
compensating. So the first work is identification: can you feel it, can you make it contract
on purpose, can you tell it apart from its neighbours.

The corpus has the drill-level version. *"Squeeze your armpits down toward your hips... If
you can't feel it, the muscle is deeply inhibited — keep trying daily until you can find
it."* (Footwear of Peace) And *"the lifts will be close to impossible at first. That is the
point — it is a connection you do not have yet."* (`bl-toes`)

**And per Part 2, sometimes the reason you cannot find it is not neural at all.** A lower
core held stretched by a distended abdomen is not inhibited, it is mechanically
disadvantaged — and no amount of trying to find it fixes that. Empty out, then look.

### F2 · Proprioception is trainable, and it is the point
**id** `proprioception`

> This directly attacks "my brain never got the memo."
> **[quoted — Tailored Movement Program]**

Position sense is measurable, it degrades, and it comes back with training. The eyes-closed
single-leg test is the instrument — closing the eyes removes the sense that was quietly
doing all the work — and the ladder runs eyes-open → eyes-closed → head turns → unstable
surface. *"Only move up a level when the current one is boring."* (`bl-prog`)

> Your foot will be visibly working — toes gripping, arch adjusting constantly. That is the
> foot-to-brain conversation you are trying to restart. **[quoted — `bl-prog`]**

*Always within reach of a counter, for anyone with an orthostatic picture.*

### F3 · Notice is the instrument
**id** `notice-is-the-instrument`

Every one of the 33 cards carries a **notice** field, and it is how you find out whether the
thing worked without a device: *"the ground feels more detailed"* · *"Ribs stop flaring"* ·
*"The urge to crack drops"* · *"Standing tall stops being effort."* Learning to read those
is the deepest skill the app teaches.

**[synthesis — still flagged.]** It is on every card and named as a principle nowhere. Quiet
centre of the method, or a field name promoted to a philosophy? Kevin's call.

### F4 · One number, honestly taken
**id** `one-real-measure`

> The number rising over weeks IS the thing you are trying to fix, and it is the only hard
> metric in this document. **[quoted — `br-co2` card]**

Feel drifts; a few measures do not. Knee-to-wall, eyes-closed balance seconds, single-leg
calf raises, breath-hold tolerance — same time of day, same conditions, written down, every
three to four weeks. *"Use numbers, not feelings."* (Tailored Movement Program) And the
discipline is in the taking: *"If you are gasping afterwards you held too long and the number
is invalid."*

### F5 · Where it hurts is not where it is from
**id** `referral`

> If your hamstring range improves from this when stretching has not moved it, the
> restriction was never muscular. **[quoted — `nv-sciatic` card]**

The practical form is a test, not a theory: try the other tissue's tool and see whether the
thing moves. Nerve is its own tissue with its own rules — *"this is flossing, not stretching
— never hold the end position... More is not better here."*

## Part 6 — Dose, safety, and time

### D1 · Most of this is uncomfortable, and that is the work
**id** `pain-is-the-medium`

Rolling out the back of the armpit is intense; you will hit a nerve and feel it run down the
arm. For the people this is aimed at, pain is not an exception to daily life — it *is* daily
life. So the honest pitch is not gentleness, it is that the discomfort buys something: easier
breathing, the weight of the head coming off the neck, joint pain slowly dissipating, feeling
more free.

**The app never decides for you which pain is which.** Nobody can call that for someone
else's body, and an app that warned before every item would be ignored before every item.
Teach the difference once and properly, put the honest caution on the card where the risk is
real, and deal the safer work first. *(Ruling R25.)*

### D2 · The signals that mean stop are specific
**id** `stop-signals`

Not "it hurts." These:

- **Sharp**, as against deep and intense. *"It should never be sharp."* (`rb-diaph`)
- **Numbness, tingling or shooting** down a limb — reposition or reduce range, never push.
- **A joint giving way, sliding or catching** — a stability problem, not something to push
  through.
- **Lightheadedness** — stop, sit, breathe normally.
- **Joint pain as against muscle fatigue.** *"Jaw fatigue is expected; jaw pain is not."*
- **Swelling, locking, or anything that feels like instability rather than tightness** — see
  somebody. (Body Work Manual, Part 4)

### D3 · Dose is the safety mechanism
**id** `dose-is-safety`

> Start at half the volume you think you need and watch the following two days before adding
> any. **[quoted — `rg-ecc` card]**

The choice is almost never do-it-or-don't; it is how much, how hard, how often — which is
where safety actually lives, because it leaves the thing available. *"Daily and light beats
hard and occasional."* (`feet`) *"Patience over intensity."* and *"Small and consistent beats
big and sporadic."* (Tailored Movement Program)

### D4 · Delayed is the operative word
**id** `delayed-response`

> Eccentrics cause real delayed soreness, and delayed is the operative word given the
> post-exertional picture. **[quoted — `rg-ecc` card, generalised]**

The cost of a session does not always arrive during the session. That breaks the ordinary
feedback loop everybody learns to train by, and what replaces it is a longer look-back and a
written record — which is the whole reason the app keeps one.

### D5 · Say what a fair trial looks like before it starts
**id** `fair-test-up-front`

Some of this moves in a session. Some moves in a quarter — *"Effects on sleep take months,
not days — this is the slowest-acting thing in this document and also one of the
highest-value."* (`aw-press`) If the app does not say which up front, "I tried it and it
didn't work" has no honest answer; and for people told for years that they did not try hard
enough, an unfalsifiable promise is not sloppy, it is cruel. *(Content law 3.)*

### D6 · A default you are rebuilding, not a rule you are passing
**id** `data-not-failure`

> Reaching for the mouth constantly is data, not failure — it points at nasal obstruction.
> **[quoted — `br-nasal`]**

Everything off-plan is information about the system. A missed day is not a debt; an empty
bottle is a reason to stop asking; a break in nose breathing is a pointer at an airway. *"It
is a default you are rebuilding, not a rule you are passing or failing."*

## Part 7 — The arc

### A1 · Conscious first, automatic later
**id** `conscious-to-automatic`

> What begins as concerted, conscious effort becomes baseline. The app's success is measured
> by how much of itself becomes unnecessary. **[quoted — FRAMEWORK]**

This is the shape of the whole programme, and v1 left it out. Every postural and breathing
default in the body was learned, and the broken ones are held in place by a nervous system
running hot. So the plan is: do it on purpose, over and over, while also lowering the
system's baseline — and it holds on its own as the autonomic state settles. *"That is the
resting position. Everything else in this section is training you toward holding it without
thinking about it."* (`aw-posture`)

Two honesty notes ride with it. The claim is held at the level of movement patterns and
posture; the deeper autonomic half is the working hypothesis. And structural limits are real
— where an airway is narrowed by anatomy, breath work strengthens the muscles and
re-establishes the pattern but does not rebuild the passage. Saying so is part of what makes
the rest trustworthy.

### A2 · Everyday positions are the reps
**id** `positions-are-reps`

The set is not where most of the work happens. How you stand at the counter, how you sit,
where your tongue rests, whether your mouth is closed — those are hours a day against a few
minutes of exercise. The awareness layer is trained, not scheduled: stack up, reverse the
joint, squeeze and hold, belly button to spine.

### A3 · Hang it on something you already do
**id** `attach-to-a-ritual`

> Fold it into brushing your teeth so you do not have to remember it separately.
> **[quoted — `aw-palate`]**

The failure mode of every home programme is remembering it. So attach the work to a cue that
already exists and colonise the dead time around it.

### A4 · Shore up the weakest link
**id** `weakest-link`

> In personal development you lean into your strengths. In the body it is reversed — you
> shore up the weakest links so the whole system can get strong.
> **[derived — Kevin, Aug 20]**

The chain performs at the level of its worst link, and the strong parts are already
compensating for the weak ones, which is what made them tight. Training what you are already
good at just makes the compensation better.

### A5 · The foundation never retires
**id** `foundation-never-retires`

> You're not rehabbing back to zero and stopping. You're building a body that performs and
> lasts. Each phase is the prerequisite for the next, and the foundation never fully retires
> — it becomes lifelong maintenance. **[quoted — Tailored Movement Program, generalised]**

Foundation and resilience · stability and capacity · strength · performance. You graduate on
evidence, not on a calendar, and roughly twice a week of the foundation stays in the
programme for life. Where laxity is structural, that maintenance *is* the thing buying the
pain-free decades.

## Part 8 — What is thin, and needs Kevin

Named rather than papered over.

1. **Fascia (M7).** The largest hole. Named as co-equal with muscle work everywhere and
   worked out nowhere. Needs: how fascial work differs in practice, what "reconnection"
   means, how a fascial restriction presents differently, timescales.
2. **Lymphatic work.** Kevin named *"fascia/lymph fluid backup"* around the lats. The
   roadmap references a lymphatic doc that is not on this machine — either it is in the
   claude.ai Project or it needs writing. Dry brushing currently ships as a daily-support
   item with no teaching behind it at all.
3. **Breath as a pneumatic force (M3).** Drafted from three sentences. Needs the details.
4. **Skeletal realignment.** Kevin named it; the corpus holds the pieces — rib position,
   pelvic tilt, femoral rotation, arch height, head over shoulders — but never states
   alignment as an outcome with a method and a way to check it.
5. **The gut → core sequence as a protocol.** Part 2 has the mechanism. What it lacks is the
   order of operations: what "empty out" means in practice, which drills identify the lower
   core once there is room, and how to tell a core that is inhibited from one that is
   mechanically blocked.
6. **The education backlog.** Chewing · motility's ignition (the dog in the backyard) · the
   capacity loop with its safety gate in the same breath · gut conditions. All named in the
   roadmap, none written.

## What happens to this next

Once these are Kevin's rather than mine they become `src/content/principles.json` — id,
name, statement, teaching — and every catalogue item gains a `principleIds` list. The app
renders "why this is here" from the linked principles instead of from a form cue, one edit to
a principle improves every item pointing at it, and the composer finally gets a real answer
to "what is this item *for*."

Two structural notes for that step, both of which came out of v2 rather than v1:

- **The chain in Part 2 is not a principle, it is a graph.** Items should point at a *link*
  in it — "this is the lats-anchor-the-ribs link" — because that is what lets a connection
  card say why this comes before that.
- **Part 6 is the safety layer and should be addressable on its own**, since R25 orders the
  on-ramp by risk, and that ordering needs risk to be a property of the item rather than a
  paragraph in a document.

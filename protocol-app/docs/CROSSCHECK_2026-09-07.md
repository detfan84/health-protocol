# The food figures, cross-checked — and polyphenols, finally measured

*7 Sep 2026. The promised two-source pass: every checkable figure verified
against an independent national dataset, and the polyphenol gap filled from the
database that actually measures them. Changes applied; this is the record.*

## The verification — UK CoFID vs our USDA figures

207 of the 247 figures could be matched against CoFID 2021 (McCance &
Widdowson). 110 agreed within ±30% — the expected band for different labs
sampling different varieties. Nearly all the rest are **known systematic
dataset differences**, not errors: UK folate assays read lower than USDA's
method across the board, CoFID's 2021 re-analysis raised B12 on animal foods,
selenium tracks soil, and carotene (vitamin A in plants) varies several-fold by
variety — US sweet potatoes and squash are simply more orange than UK ones.
Ten more flags were product mismatches on the CoFID side (their "Greek style"
yogurt is unstrained; their "plain" chocolate is ~40% cocoa).

**One figure was genuinely wrong, and it was ours: tahini magnesium.** USDA's
default tahini entry says 95 mg/100 g — chemically implausible, since hulling
sesame removes calcium (which lives in the hull) but not magnesium (in the
kernel). Whole sesame is ~350–370 everywhere, CoFID's tahini says 380, and
USDA's own unroasted-kernel tahini entry says 353. The mapping moved to that
entry. A handful of pumpkin seeds keeps its magnesium crown; a tablespoon of
tahini now honestly carries ~53 mg instead of ~14.

**One worth knowing, not fixable from USDA:** their only smoked-salmon entry
is lean chinook (0.45 g omega-3/100 g). The smoked salmon people actually buy
is fatty Atlantic, nearer 2 g. The card now says so rather than silently
underclaiming fourfold. Also noted on record: brazil-nut selenium spans
~100–4000 µg by soil, and beef-liver vitamin A can run 3× our figure by animal
age — both figures stand, with the variability acknowledged here.

## Polyphenols — out of the "unmeasurable" list

USDA's polyphenol column is empty for every food, which is why the card said
"nothing honest to print." Phenol-Explorer v3.6 (the dataset behind
Pérez-Jiménez 2010) measures them by Folin assay, and **16 figures now ride on
the rows whose form matches** — each marked `Phenol-Explorer 3.6` with its
assay basis, never masquerading as USDA. Cocoa powder tops the shelf at 5624
mg/100 g; two squares of dark chocolate carry ~370 mg; a cup of brewed green
tea ~150 mg.

**Three rows stay blank, and that's the form rule holding across sources:**
buckwheat (they measured dry flour; we serve cooked groats), pomegranate (they
measured juice; we serve arils), tinned tomatoes (no entry). The named-gap test
grew to nine and still names every one exactly.

*Sourcing note: phenol-explorer.eu currently serves an unrelated database from
its domain; the official composition file was retrieved from the Internet
Archive. Worth knowing if anyone re-verifies.*

## Still unmeasurable, now five

Nitrate (USDA's column is empty; Frida or a literature pass could fill it
later) · probiotics (organisms, not amounts) · collagen · creatine · melatonin.

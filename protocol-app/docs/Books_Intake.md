## The line that matters more here than with reels

A reel is a claim with a face attached (Reels_Intake). A book is that AND
somebody's commercial product, so the standing rulings apply with less slack:

- **Learn from, never transcribe.** The book's text, photographs and
  program-as-written cannot be reproduced. What can enter: principles, in the
  app's own words, attributed, with epistemic status carried on the claim
  (law 5) — and techniques Kevin actually adopts into his own practice,
  authored from HIS notes the way reels are.
- **Whose content it is** (inherited from Reels_Intake / FRAMEWORK): anything
  extracted is Kevin's imported content, not shipped catalogue. Shipping
  derived material under the app's name is a separate decision, unmade — and
  if it is ever made, what ships must be the app's own words and its own
  program, with the book as a cited influence, never a copied source.
- **Tier:** `exploratory` by default, `sourceNote` naming book and page.
  Where the book cites research the note can say so; the tier does not rise
  on the book's own say-so.
- **Nutrition claims** go through the Food shelf's own law: measured figures
  from named sources, no invented numbers, no warning theatre. A book chapter
  is an index of things to check, never a source of figures.

## Division of labour — the medium's rule, again

Same as reels, and books are the easier medium: stable, page-numbered, no
export dance. Kevin reads and dumps rough notes — "BFB ch 7, sled drags,
want this as a levels ladder" is a complete note — and CC authors catalogue
items, or Learn material for his strikes. Nothing enters unflagged; the app
never mines a book on its own.

## When to start

When Kevin hands the first notes, from either medium. The ten-first shape
test (Reels_Intake) remains the cheap way to prove the note-to-item
conversion before committing to a sweep — and it counts the same whether the
ten come from a book, the reels, or both.
